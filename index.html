01

Question and audience

Research question. In the May 2020 issue of Văn học và Tuổi trẻ (“Literature and Youth”, a national magazine for secondary-school students), what vocabulary do teacher-written creative pieces and student essays use to represent the school year, and how much does each group's writing register the events of early 2020?

  1. Themes: what does each group write about most?
  2. Time: does each group look back, or at the present?
  3. School: what does "going to school" look like in each?

Audience. We write for three groups.

  • Literature teachers and secondary students who use this magazine as model writing. They need to know whether these model texts reflect students' real lives, and whose voice students are being taught to write in.
  • Our own generation: people who were in high school in 2020 and lived through the same school closures, online lessons and strange summer. For them, this is a chance to see their own year on the page again, with a little nostalgia, and to compare it with how adults wrote about that time.
  • The magazine's editors and anyone studying how Vietnam remembers the Covid-19 school year. This issue has no digital copy online; our files make its pages searchable and show whose voices the magazine chose to print.

Why this issue. Schools closed in February 2020; most students returned in May. This rare double issue sits right at that moment, and we found no digital copy of it online.

Why us. We, the people who made this project, lived through that same summer: in those years we were high-school students ourselves. So we came to this issue with curiosity, and a little nostalgia.

05

Three decisions that changed the results

1. Joining compound words for Voyant

Problem
Voyant splits Vietnamese by syllable: tin giả (fake news) becomes tin (news) + giả (fake).
Alternative
Leave the text unjoined, which over-counts tin (news, 29) and học (study, 20), because they are also parts of longer words.
Trade-off
Joining fixes those counts but scatters dịch (epidemic) into đại_dịch (pandemic), dịch_bệnh (epidemic disease), nghỉ_dịch (epidemic break), mùa_dịch (epidemic season). The pandemic almost vanishes from the word cloud.
What we did
Joined ~250 compounds from a checked list, counted the pandemic as a word group, and kept an unjoined corpus for dịch (epidemic) and mùa (season).
"dịch" (epidemic) standing alone in student texts23 → 2unjoined → joined

2. Removing the essay prompts from the student corpus

Problem
The p. 94 prompt already says Covid-19, virus corona, nghỉ học (time off school), trải nghiệm ý nghĩa (meaningful experiences) and thay đổi tích cực (positive change). Those are adult words.
Alternative
Keep the prompts, which inflates the students' pandemic count with language they did not write.
Trade-off
We lose a direct count of how much the prompt shapes the essay, so we compare the prompts with the essays by close reading (Finding 3).

3. Keeping the magazine's print errors

Problem
The magazine prints đối vợi, bất đầu, ap dụng, giẫy giụa (misspellings of đối với “towards”, bắt đầu “begin”, áp dụng “apply”, giãy giụa “struggle”), and calls the virus vi khuẩn (bacterium).
Alternative
Correct them, so Voyant counts với (with, towards) once instead of splitting it.
Trade-off
Faithful text, and a clear AI test: DeepAI silently "fixed" three of the four spellings despite the prompt. Cost: four words counted separately, none in our word groups.

1 · Joining compound words. We joined compound words because single syllables do not show what the writer means or feels. Tin (news) and giả (fake) on their own say nothing; only tin giả (fake news) carries the writer's concern. But joining had a cost: dịch (epidemic) fell from 23 to 2, because it was split into đại_dịch, dịch_bệnh, nghỉ_dịch, mùa_dịch (pandemic, epidemic disease, epidemic break, epidemic season) and others, so the pandemic almost disappeared from the word cloud. So we did both: we used the joined text for the top terms, added related words together into word groups, and kept an unjoined copy to check dịch (epidemic) and mùa (season) in context.

2 · Removing the essay prompts. We removed the prompts because they were written by adults. If we had left them in, adult words such as Covid-19, nghỉ học (time off school) and thay đổi tích cực (positive change) would be counted as the students' words, and our judgement about the students would not be objective. Keeping the prompts aside also showed us something: the students answer in the shape the prompt sets, so they had to write inside an adult mould.

3 · Keeping the print errors. We kept the print errors because we wanted the text to stay as close to the original as possible. Using AI to transcribe can improve the text and help preserve it, but that "improvement" is also the problem: the AI corrects things on its own and becomes inaccurate. DeepAI changed the misprints vợi, bất đầu, ap dụng to the correct spelling even though the prompt told it not to, and we only saw this because we checked every difference against the printed page. What we learned: using AI is necessary and convenient for preserving texts, but it has to be used flexibly and carefully, so that the AI does not make its own subjective corrections.

06

Findings

1 · The two groups write about two different Mays

Summer and nature make up 85 of every 1,000 teacher words and none of the student words. The pandemic and technology together make up 38 of every 1,000 student words and none of the teacher words.

Even the shared word mùa (season) splits: mùa hạ, mùa hè (both mean summer) for the teachers, mùa dịch (the epidemic season) twice for the students.

So what the two groups feel the summer of 2020 in completely opposite ways. The teachers write about summer in its pure form, which is sometimes less objective than the account of the people who lived through it. The students write about what they actually went through: the epidemic season. Tin_giả (fake news) leads their words because fake news was a pressing problem at that time. We think the adults left the pandemic out to avoid the negative feelings and confusion of those months, a Covid season that was deeply tragic.

The split in mùa says the same. For the teachers, mùa hạ and mùa hè (summer) are a season to enjoy, to play and to remember the old days. The students write mùa dịch (the epidemic season): it is still summer, but they do not feel it the way adults do; it is more like a war.

2 · Students live a different summer from the one adults remember

Nostalgia words are 3.7 times more frequent in the teacher texts (14.5 vs 3.9 per 1,000). The same word nhớ means "remember" for the teachers and "miss" for the students.

"Giờ nhớ lại thấy lòng mình nôn nao khó tả."“Remembering it now, I feel a stirring inside I cannot describe.”Teacher, Những chú ve (The Cicadas), p. 85: remembering childhood
"tôi hơi hụt hẫng vì nhớ trường, nhớ lớp."“I felt a little lost because I missed school, missed my class.”Grade 8 student, p. 94: missing school during the closure
"Tôi lập kế hoạch cụ thể từng giờ, từng việc trong ngày."“I made a concrete plan for every hour and every task of the day.”Grade 8 student, p. 95: time as a timetable

So what the students are living a summer very different from the one the adults remember. The same word nhớ shows it. For the adults, nhớ is nostalgia, thinking back to an innocent time. For the students, it is a real wish to go back to school, and the feeling of being shut in while the country was in lockdown.

3 · Whose words do the students use?

The teachers' school is classic imagery: học_trò (pupils), áo_trắng (the white school uniform), bài_giảng của thầy (the teacher's lesson), tan_trường (the end of the school day). The students' school has no uniform and no classroom: tự_học (self-study, 5), bài_tập (homework, 3), sách_giáo_khoa (textbooks, 2), video, online lessons. During the epidemic, students quickly gained access to technology, which was a big shift.

But the frame is adult. The Grade 8 essay answers its prompt's "meaningful experiences" and "positive change" almost word for word, ending "Cảm ơn kì nghỉ dịch" (“Thank you, epidemic holiday”). The Grade 11 essay borrows the war language of the 2020 public campaign: giặc dịch bệnh (the epidemic as an invading enemy), chiến sĩ (soldiers), tuyến đầu (the front line), hậu phương (the home front), quyết thắng đại dịch (determined to defeat the pandemic) (16 war-related words; none in GV).

So what the students' slogan-like language comes from adults, from the moulds adults set, which the students learn and imitate.

We think all three possible explanations play a part: who is writing, the genre and the prompts the students were given, and how the magazine sorts its texts into sections.

07

Limitations

  • Tiny, uneven sample: five texts, about 2,800 words, one issue. Những chú ve (The Cicadas) is about 77% of the teacher group, so ve (cicada) is really one text's word.
  • Writer and genre are tangled: all teacher texts are poems or lyrical prose; both student texts answer set prompts.
  • Selected texts: these are what the editors chose to print. We cannot see what was rejected, or when each text was written.
  • Our categories: we decided which words count as "nostalgia" or "school". Voyant counts both meanings of nhớ (remember / miss) as one word.
  • Tools: results depend on our compound list. OCR lost drop capitals; DeepAI corrected and guessed silently; poems lose their line breaks in Voyant.
  • Voyant mirror: voyant-tools.org was down on 24 Oct, so we used the official LINCS mirror (same version, 2.6.22).
08

Conclusion

In this issue, teachers and students feel the summer of 2020 in completely opposite ways. The teachers write a pure summer of cicadas and sunlight. The students write the summer they actually went through: the epidemic season of online lessons, timetables and fake news.

The students' words also come partly from adults: from the prompts they were set and from the slogans they learned and imitated. So when teachers evaluate students' writing, they should put themselves in the position of the person who is actually living the experience. That means respecting the students' real experience and judging how they actually write, not which "summer" sounds nicer or more vivid.
